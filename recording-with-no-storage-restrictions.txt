To automatically record calls from a custom softphone, you generally need open-source tools that can either capture your system's audio channels or act as a proxy/server for your telephony traffic.
Here are 4 open-source options to record your cold calls for free:

1. Asterisk

• How it works: Asterisk is a powerful, open-source PBX (Private Branch Exchange) platform. Instead of recording directly on your computer, you route your custom softphone calls through an Asterisk server.
• Recording capability: It features a built-in MixMonitor() application designed specifically to record two-way audio conversations automatically and save them as WAV or MP3 files.
• Best for: Users who want a centralized, server-side recording solution that handles calls before they even reach the endpoint.

2. FreeSWITCH

• How it works: FreeSWITCH is a software-defined telecom platform designed to route and interconnect popular communication protocols (like SIP and WebRTC).
• Recording capability: It has native, high-performance session recording modules. You can configure it to automatically trigger call recording the moment a outbound cold call connects.
• Best for: Developers running custom softphones who need a highly scalable, programmatic backend that can handle hundreds of concurrent recordings without dropping audio quality.

3. OBS Studio (Open Broadcaster Software)

• How it works: While known for video streaming, OBS Studio is an incredibly reliable open-source tool for advanced audio routing and capturing on local machines.
• Recording capability: You can configure OBS to capture specific desktop audio outputs (your prospect's voice) and microphone inputs (your voice) into separate audio tracks. It can run in the background and record continuously.
• Best for: A quick, client-side setup if your custom softphone outputs audio through standard system sound devices and you don't want to manage a server.

4. SIP3

• How it works: SIP3 is an open-source traffic analysis and monitoring platform tailored for VoIP service providers and large-scale voice architectures.
• Recording capability: It works by sniffing network packets. If your custom softphone uses standard SIP/RTP protocols, SIP3 can intercept the RTP packets (the actual audio data packets traveling over your network) and reconstruct them into playable audio files.
• Best for: Advanced setups where you want to record calls passively at the network level without altering the softphone application or server configuration.

To record every call from your custom softphone automatically and for free, you can integrate open-source backend recording solutions or packet sniffers that capture the voice streams directly from your network or server.
Here are 4 open-source options suited for this setup:

1. Oreka (OrkAudio)

• How it works: Oreka is one of the most mature open-source (GPL) enterprise-grade voice recording projects. Its modular component, OrkAudio, acts as a daemon that can passively sniff network traffic to extract and record VoIP RTP sessions without altering your call flow.
• Key Features: It tracks SIP Call-IDs, supports modern codecs (G.711, Opus, G.729), and includes a web-based user interface called Orkweb to search, download, and replay recordings.
• Best for: Out-of-the-box passive network recording with a dedicated playback interface.

2. VoIPmonitor

• How it works: VoIPmonitor is primarily an open-source network packet sniffer designed to analyze the quality of VoIP calls. However, it comes with built-in capabilities to fully decode audio streams from SIP, RTP, and WebRTC packets.
• Key Features: It can automatically save each intercepted call as a standard .wav or .pcap audio file along with its metadata (caller ID, callee, timestamp). It scales easily to handle thousands of concurrent calls seamlessly.
• Best for: Developers looking to silently capture audio directly from network packets while monitoring call quality metrics.

3. OpenSIPS (via SIPREC)

• How it works: If your custom softphone routes through a SIP proxy or server, OpenSIPS is a robust signaling engine that natively implements the SIPREC protocol (RFC 7245). It acts as a Session Recording Client (SRC) that securely duplicates and forwards call audio to a recording server.
• Key Features: Highly programmable routing scripts, massive throughput, and clean metadata tagging. You can configure it to instantly fork calls to an external recording sink like Oreka.
• Best for: Complex server-side automation where you want to dictate exactly when a call recording starts or stops based on database rules.

4. Drachtio SIPREC Recording Server

• How it works: Drachtio SIPREC Server is an open-source framework built specifically to receive SIPREC metadata and media flows. It leverages rtpengine or media servers like FreeSWITCH under the hood to process and dump incoming audio tracks into audio files.
• Key Features: It accurately parses multipart SDP/XML metadata sent from an SBC or proxy and pairs the corresponding audio channels together dynamically.
• Best for: Node.js/JavaScript-centric environments looking for a lightweight, modern stack to aggregate incoming SIPREC recording streams.
