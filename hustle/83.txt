The core idea in that quote is **not really "be addicted to your phone."** The useful business principle underneath it is:

> **Turn your phone into a revenue-generation device instead of a consumption device.**

For your business (AI phone agents for martial arts schools), that's actually a very applicable concept if implemented intelligently.

The mistake would be internalizing:

> "I should stare at my phone all day."

The useful version is:

> "Whenever I have available attention, my phone should present me with opportunities to create, sell, follow up, market, improve, or close deals."

# What This Looks Like Specifically for AI Phone Agents

If you fully internalized the useful part of that quote, your phone behavior would look like this:

Every time you unlock your phone, you ask:

> "Can I create revenue, create content, improve the product, or strengthen a relationship in the next 2 minutes?"

Examples:

* DM a martial arts school owner.
* Follow up with a lead.
* Record a quick video.
* Capture a feature idea.
* Post an insight.
* Reply to a prospect.
* Review call transcripts.
* Ask a client for a testimonial.
* Check missed opportunities.
* Brainstorm new automations.
* Improve onboarding.

That is the business version of the philosophy.

The winning takeaway isn't "be addicted to your phone."

It's:

> **Be addicted to creating value and moving opportunities forward, and make your phone the fastest possible tool for doing that.**

For an AI phone-agent company serving martial arts schools, that can easily translate into dozens of additional touches, content pieces, follow-ups, and product improvements every week—all of which compound into more clients over time.
