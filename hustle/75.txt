## The actual mechanism behind the rant

The rant is right, but it's right for a reason it doesn't fully explain. Here's the reason: **political attention has a leverage curve, and you're on the flat part of it.**

Amazon spends on politics because a single tariff line, a single antitrust ruling, a single state tax deal is worth nine figures to them. Their political attention converts into money. Steel companies lobbied for tariffs that were essentially written to their spec. Pharma fights Medicare price negotiation because a rule change moves billions. For those firms, politics *is* a revenue activity.

You sell AI phone agents to martial arts schools. Nothing you think about the election converts into anything. Your political attention has a conversion rate of zero. It's not that politics doesn't matter — it's that *your* politics doesn't matter, to your P&L, at your size. That's the whole argument.

## Run the math so it stops being an opinion

Say you're at 25 schools at $400/month. That's $10k MRR, $120k/year, maybe $80k profit.

- **Tax goes up 2%.** You lose $1,600/year. That's $133/month. One-third of one customer.
- **You add one school.** You gain $4,800/year. One new logo absorbs three tax hikes.
- **You raise price to $450.** That's $15,000/year — nine tax hikes, from an email.
- **You cut monthly churn from 5% to 3%.** At 25 schools compounding over a year, that's the difference between ending at 40 schools and ending at 28. Worth 20x the tax change.
- **You stop losing two deals a month to bad follow-up.** $800 MRR, $9,600/year.

Every single lever on that list is bigger than the political variable, and every single one is fully inside your control. That's the thing to burn into your head: the controllable levers are *larger* than the uncontrollable ones. Not equal. Larger.

## Why it's specifically a trap, not just a waste

A waste is watching TV. A trap is something that *feels like work*. Politics-talk is dangerous because it wears the costume of business strategy:

- "I'm waiting to see what happens with the election before I hire." That's not analysis, that's a permission slip to not hire.
- "Once the economy turns, martial arts schools will have more budget." Schools that are full have budget now. Schools that are empty didn't have budget in any economy.
- "Tax policy is going to determine my entity structure." You're doing $10k MRR. Your entity structure is a 40-minute conversation with an accountant, once.
- "AI regulation is going to kill this whole space." Then go read the actual rules for 90 minutes and act on them — see below — instead of vibing about it for a year.

Here's the filter: **did this change what I do Monday morning?** If the answer is no, it was entertainment with a serious face on.

## Where the rant is wrong, and it matters for your business specifically

Do not confuse *politics* with *regulation in your vertical*. They feel similar and they're completely different.

Politics = who won, what the tax rate is, what the culture war is doing this week. Zero effect on you. Ignore totally.

Regulation in your lane = a product requirement with a lawyer attached. For AI phone agents, that's real and unavoidable:

- **Call recording consent.** Some jurisdictions require all parties to consent. This is a line in your agent's opening script, not a political opinion.
- **AI disclosure.** A growing number of rules require the caller to know they're talking to a bot. That's a feature and a sales objection you need an answer to, today.
- **Outbound calling rules.** If your agent ever dials out rather than just answering, you're in a much heavier regulatory zone than inbound. Know which one you're selling.
- **Data handling for minors.** Martial arts schools are full of kids. Your agent is collecting names, ages, and parent contact info. That's a real compliance surface.

Treat that as operations. Handle it once, put it in the product and the contract, move on. It's not politics — it's the same category as "does my payment processor work."

Also worth saying plainly: "poor people are unimportant" is a line about attention allocation dressed up as a claim about human worth, and it isn't one you have to adopt to use the rest of the framework. You can vote, care, donate, whatever — the argument here is only about where your *business* energy goes on a Tuesday afternoon.

## Implementation

**1. Set the threshold in writing.** The rant says "until you're a multimillionaire." Make it a number. Mine would be: *when a 1% tax change equals one month's revenue, I'll pay attention to tax policy.* At $10k MRR on $80k profit, that's when profit hits about $1M. Write the number down. Until then, the topic is closed, and it's closed by a rule, not by willpower.

**2. Cut the intake, not the opinion.** You won't stop caring by deciding to stop caring. You stop by removing the feed. Unfollow the political accounts, kill the news app notifications, leave the group chat that's 80% politics. Replace with three martial arts school owner communities and one sales/ops feed. Your inputs determine what your brain chews on at 11pm.

**3. Build the replacement scoreboard.** Energy doesn't disappear when you stop spending it — it goes somewhere. Give it a target. One number on a whiteboard: demos booked this week. Or schools live. Or MRR. When you catch yourself spiraling about a headline, the physical move is to look at that number and ask which action moves it.

**4. Handle it on sales calls, because it will come up.** Martial arts school owners are opinionated people who spend all day talking. One of them will absolutely go on a political tangent mid-demo. You need a reflex, not a reaction:

- Owner rants about the economy → "Yeah, it's a lot. Which is exactly why I want to show you this — how many calls came in last month that nobody picked up?"
- Owner asks what you think about [politician] → "Honestly I've got my head so far down building this thing I've barely followed it. Let me show you what the agent does at 7pm on a Tuesday when you're on the mat."
- Owner assumes you agree and keeps going → Warm, brief, redirect: "Ha, I hear you. So — walk me through what happens right now when a parent calls during class."

Never agree, never disagree, never correct. Every school owner is a potential $400/month customer regardless of how they vote, and you have no business leaving money on the table over a topic you already decided doesn't matter.

**5. Audit weekly.** Friday, one question: *how many hours did I spend on things I control versus things I don't?* Controllable is demos, calls, onboarding, product, follow-up. Uncontrollable is everything else. If the ratio is worse than 9:1, you drifted.

The compressed version: **you're too small to be affected and too small to have influence, which means political attention is a pure cost with no offsetting benefit.** Revisit at a specific dollar number. Until then, the work is the schools, the calls, and the churn rate.
