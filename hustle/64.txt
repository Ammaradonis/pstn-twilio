## The mental model underneath it

He's not saying "create urgency." He's saying something more specific: **the need has to already be bleeding.** Not "you will have a problem," but "you have this problem, it happened last night, and it's happening again tonight."

Look at what he actually did with T2. The client's fear was competitors getting on TV. That's a future fear. He didn't invent anything — he moved the clock: *your competitors are booking slots right now, and you're deciding to be second.* Same with the car guy. Fuel savings, tax, insurance: those all start on the first tank, not in year three. The self-drive system helps on the commute tomorrow morning.

So the drill is: take every benefit you have and ask **"when does this start paying, and when did the pain start hurting?"** If the answer is "eventually," you don't have a pitch yet.

## Why this is a gift in your niche

You picked a business where the immediate need is almost embarrassingly easy to find. A martial arts school owner is on the mat teaching kids' class from 4:30 to 8:00pm. That is the exact window when a parent, home from work, finally googles "karate near me" and calls three schools.

He can't answer. He's holding pads. The phone is in the office on silent. The parent doesn't leave a voicemail — nobody does anymore — she calls the next school, books a trial there, and that kid trains somewhere else for the next three years.

**That happened to him yesterday. It's happening again in four hours.** That's your entire business in one sentence, and it's already true before you ever pick up the phone.

## Converting future pitches into present bleeding

Here's the translation table. Left column is what most AI sales people say. Right column is the same product.

| Future (dead) | Immediate (sells) |
|---|---|
| "This will help you scale to 300 students" | "You missed 11 calls last week during class. At your tuition, that's about $19,000 in lifetime value that walked to the gym on Route 9." |
| "You'll never miss a lead again" | "Your 6:40pm caller tonight gets a human-sounding answer and a booked trial instead of a voicemail beep." |
| "It pays for itself over time" | "It pays for itself the first time one kid signs up. One. Everything after that is profit, and it's live by Thursday." |
| "Great for when you open your second location" | "You're teaching five classes tonight. Who's answering the phone between 4:30 and 8?" |
| "Automates your front desk" | "Your front desk girl left at 5. Every call after 5 is you, on the mat, ignoring it." |
| "Improves lead response time" | "You're paying for Facebook ads right now. Those leads came in at 9pm. Nobody called them back. You bought them and threw them out." |

Notice the pattern: **every immediate version has a timestamp in it.** Last week. Tonight. At 6:40. Right now. If you can't put a clock on it, it's a future pitch wearing a costume.

## Make them discover it, don't announce it

This is the part he skips over but it's how the pressure actually builds. Telling someone they have an urgent problem creates resistance. Making them *see* it creates panic. A few ways to do it in your niche:

- **Call their school during class time before the meeting.** Record it. Let it ring out to voicemail. Play that recording back to them in the meeting and say nothing. Let the silence do the work. That's their prospect's experience, in their own voice, on their own greeting.
- **Ask them to open their own phone.** "Pull up your call log. Scroll to last Tuesday between 4 and 8. How many of those did you call back?" They discover it themselves, which means they can't argue with it.
- **Show their Google Business Profile call data.** Clicks on "call" versus what they remember answering.
- **Ask about the leads they already paid for.** "How many Meta leads came in last month? What time do they usually come in? Who called them back, and how fast?" Every school owner has a graveyard of form fills nobody touched.
- **Let the agent answer *them*.** Give them the number, have them call it as a nervous parent. When the thing handles the objection about their five-year-old being shy and books a trial, the sale is basically done.

## Your immediate-need inventory

Keep this list in your head so you can grab whichever one is live for the person in front of you. Each of these is happening today, not next year:

1. **Class-hour blackout.** 4:30–8pm, the owner is teaching and the phone is unmanned. Peak class time is peak call time.
2. **After-hours parent research.** Parents look at 9–11pm after bedtime and on Sunday afternoons. Nobody is at the school then.
3. **Speed-to-lead on paid ads.** They're spending money today on leads that sit for six hours. The ad spend is already gone.
4. **Front desk just quit / is 19 and terrible on the phone.** This is a fire, today, not a strategy conversation.
5. **Seasonal surge that's already started.** Back-to-school, the January New Year rush, summer camp registration. If the window is open now, being unavailable during it is a present-tense loss.
6. **A competitor who answers.** New gym opened three miles away. Call them, right there in the meeting, and see if a human picks up. If they do, you don't have to say another word.
7. **Trial no-shows and reschedules.** Confirmation calls nobody makes because nobody has time.
8. **Owner burnout.** He's answering sales calls at dinner with his kids, on the mat, at his own training. That's tonight's problem.
9. **Dormant lead list.** 400 old inquiries in their CRM that could be called this week.

## Language patterns to drill

Words that kill your pitch: *will, could, eventually, down the line, as you grow, long-term, scale, future-proof, next year.*

Words that carry it: *right now, last week, tonight, this month, already, currently, today, by Thursday.*

Some lines to keep loaded:

- "Forget next year for a second. What happened last Tuesday at 6pm when that phone rang?"
- "How many calls did you miss last week? Don't guess, look."
- "You're not choosing between this and nothing. You're choosing between this and voicemail. Voicemail closes zero percent."
- "Your competitors aren't going to answer the phone next year. They're answering it tonight."
- "One student covers this. How many did you miss in the last 30 days?"

## Objection handling: they'll always try to push it to the future

When a school owner says "let's revisit in January," he's handing you the reframe:

- **"Revisit in January."** → "January is the single biggest enrollment month you have. Every parent who made a resolution calls in the first ten days. You want this installed and warmed up before that, not during it. If we start now, January runs itself. If we start January 2nd, you've already spent the week missing the calls."
- **"After the belt test / after the tournament."** → "That's exactly when your phone rings most. Parents see the photos, they ask their friend where her kid trains, they call. You want that call going to voicemail during the one week you get free advertising?"
- **"We only get a few calls a month."** → "Then every single one is worth $1,700 to you, and missing three is $5,000. Low volume makes this *more* important, not less."
- **"My front desk handles it."** → "What time does she leave? What happens between 5 and 9? What happens Saturday morning?"
- **"Let me talk to my wife/partner."** → Fine, but send them home with the recording of their own voicemail. That's the thing that gets discussed at dinner, not your brochure.

## One implementation warning

Your pitch can only be as immediate as your delivery. If your onboarding takes four weeks, you're a future pitch no matter how you phrase it. **"Live by Thursday" has to be true.** Build your setup so you can get an agent answering their number within days, with their class schedule, their pricing, their trial offer. Time-to-first-value is the actual product here. If a school owner can say "we booked two trials off it on night one," you never have to manufacture urgency again.

## The one line I'd change

He says the immediate need works "even if they're bullshit, even if they're garbage." That works in a one-shot transactional sale where you never see the person again. It will wreck you in this niche specifically. Martial arts school owners are a tiny, tight, referral-heavy world: the same Facebook groups, the same seminars, the same association conferences, the same three guys everyone calls before buying software. If you sell invented urgency, you get one sale and then a thread about you.

And you don't need it. The real pain here is so abundant that manufacturing any is just lazy. The phone genuinely isn't being answered. The ad spend genuinely is being wasted. ## The drill to internalize it

For the next week, every time you write or say a sentence about your product, stop and ask: **"What time did this problem happen?"** If you can't answer with a day and an hour, rewrite it. Do that fifty times and you'll stop generating future pitches entirely.

The core idea in that transcript is not really "create panic." The useful principle is:

> People prioritize solving problems that are affecting them right now. Future benefits are usually weaker motivators than current pain, current risk, current cost, or current missed opportunity.

For an AI phone agent business serving martial arts schools, the mistake would be pitching:

> "In 2 years, AI will be everywhere, so you should adopt it now."

Most owners do not wake up worrying about AI adoption curves.

They wake up worrying about:

* Missed leads
* Empty classes
* Staff costs
* No-shows
* Parents not getting answers
* Administrative overload
* Competitors taking students
* Phones ringing when nobody answers

Those are immediate problems.

# The Best Discovery Questions

Before pitching, uncover immediate pain.

Ask:

* "How many calls go unanswered each week?"
* "Who answers the phone during classes?"
* "How quickly do new leads get a response?"
* "How many trial class requests came in last month?"
* "How many of those converted?"
* "What happens when someone calls after hours?"

The answers become your pitch.

For example:

Owner says:

Your pitch becomes:

> "Then the issue isn't AI. The issue is that interested parents are calling while your team is teaching. The AI agent makes sure those calls get answered immediately."
