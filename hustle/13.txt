The core of what you pasted is a real skill: estimating how a business makes money from what you can see, then spotting where it leaks. Consultants and investors do this constantly. Below I've broken the text into seven principles and translated each one into your business, with several examples for each. I've also pushed back on two parts that I think would steer you wrong.

For your business, the habit works on three levels at once:

1. **Martial arts schools**, your customers, whose economics you need to understand better than they do.
2. **Every other business's phone and customer experience**, which is free research for your AI agent.
3. **Your own competitors and industry**, meaning who else is fighting for the same school owners.

## 1. "Work out exactly WHY and HOW and WHO'S BENEFITTING"

The text says money is only exchanged, never made. So whenever money moves, trace the chain. In your world, the key exchange is a phone call turning into a membership. Your job is to understand everyone who gains or loses from that call being answered.

- **Why the parent calls.** A mom calling at 6:45pm usually isn't browsing. Her kid got bullied, or needs discipline, or she saw an Instagram ad and has a two-minute window before dinner. The *why* tells you what your agent should say first: reassurance and a booked trial, not a price list.
- **How the money flows after the call.** The call leads to a trial class, then enrollment, a monthly membership, belt testing fees, uniforms and gear, summer camps, and birthday parties. A missed call loses far more than one membership fee; it loses that whole chain.
- **Who benefits when the call is missed.** The competitor two kilometers away who *did* pick up. Say that sentence to a school owner and watch their face.
- **Who benefits when a school buys from you.** The owner gains revenue. The front desk person may feel threatened, which is a hidden objection, so position the agent as covering the hours they can't. The marketing agency running the school's Facebook ads also benefits, because their leads finally get answered and their results look better. That makes agencies potential referral partners, which is an insight you only find by asking "who benefits?"

## 2. "You don't know exactly what the rent is but you can estimate it"

This is the most valuable skill in the text. Walk into a school, or look at one online, and build a rough profit-and-loss estimate. The numbers below are illustrative; swap in your market's real figures.

- **Estimate revenue from clues.** Count names on the class schedule, kids in the lobby, and the number of Google reviews. Check the website or call for pricing. If a school looks like 150 students at $180/month, that's roughly $27,000/month.
- **Estimate rent.** Look up commercial listings nearby. A 2,500 sq ft unit in a strip mall at $20/sq ft/year is about $4,200/month. Now you know rent is roughly 15% of their revenue, and every lost student hurts.
- **Estimate the value of one call.** This is the number that sells your product. Say a student stays 18 months at $180, a lifetime value of about $3,240. If 40% of new-lead calls book a trial and 50% of trials enroll, each answered lead call is worth roughly $650. Ten missed lead calls a month is about $6,500 in lost lifetime value, every month.
- **Apply the mechanic example from your text.** The author looks at "how much they charge for labor" and "where's the big boss." At a dojo, the boss is usually on the mat teaching during the busiest call hours, 4 to 8pm. A part-time front desk person at $16/hour for 25 hours a week costs about $1,700/month and still misses calls during check-in rush. That is your price comparison.

## 3. "If I were to open a coffee shop right next door, could I beat them?"

Run this question in two directions.

**As if you were opening a competing martial arts school:**
- Could you beat the local school just by answering every call within three rings and texting a trial link within 60 seconds? Often, yes. That tells you how weak most schools' lead handling is, and that weakness is your market.
- Could you beat them with a better trial experience, say a reminder text the night before and a follow-up call after? Now you've found features your agent could handle beyond answering calls.

**As if someone opened next door to *you*:**
- A generic AI receptionist company could offer lower prices. Why would a school owner choose you? Because your agent knows the difference between a kids' program and adult BJJ, understands belt testing, and handles "is my 5-year-old too young?" well. Specialization is your moat, so keep deepening it.
- Martial arts management platforms like Kicksite or Spark Membership already own the school's data. Ask what happens if they add voice AI. Should you integrate with them, partner with them, or position against them? Better to answer that now than be surprised later.
- A local marketing agency could resell a white-label AI agent. Could you beat them, or should you become their supplier?

## 4. "Any tips or tricks they're missing?"

This is where the habit turns directly into sales ammunition. Mystery-shop martial arts schools as a prospective customer.

- **Call 20 schools at 6:30pm on a weekday**, during class. Note how many rings before an answer, whether voicemail picked up, whether the voicemail box was full, and how long any callback took.
- **Call on a Saturday afternoon or Sunday.** Many schools are closed, but parents are home and researching.
- **Fill out the website contact form and time the reply.** Hours? Days? Never?
- **Send an Instagram DM** asking about kids' classes, and track the response.
- **If someone answers, notice what they miss.** Did they ask the child's age? Book a trial on the spot? Get an email or phone number? Mention the parent's reason for calling?

Then your outreach writes itself: "I called on Tuesday evening as a parent and got voicemail. Here's what my agent would have done with that call." Keep it respectful rather than shaming, since you want them as a client. Also, just take notes during these calls; recording them without consent is illegal in the EU and in many US states.

## 5. "Analyze everything and remember everything because you're gonna use this stuff for your new company"

The text mentions websites, follow-up emails, customer support, and packaging. For you, every interaction you have with any business is research for your product and your own customer experience.

- **Every phone call you make is script research.** The pizza place that asks "want to add garlic bread?" is a model for your agent suggesting a sibling discount. The dentist that confirms by text the day before is your trial reminder flow. The airline phone menu that makes you press 0 five times is exactly what your agent must never feel like.
- **Every piece of software you sign up for is onboarding research.** If a tool made setup painless in 10 minutes, steal the structure. If one made you watch a 40-minute video first, school owners who teach six classes a day will feel the same frustration with a slow setup.
- **Every cold email or sales call you receive is outreach research.** Which ones did you actually read? Which subject lines made you delete instantly? Your school owners get pitched by marketing agencies constantly, so learn what cuts through.
- **Every invoice and cancellation flow teaches retention.** When a subscription made cancelling easy and polite, did it make you more likely to come back? When one hid the cancel button, how did you feel about the brand?

Keep a single note on your phone, a "swipe file," with three columns: what I noticed, why it worked or failed, and how I'd use it. The note is what makes the habit compound.

## 6. The "hot young girls" line: keep the question, drop the answer

I'd push back here. Hiring staff based on looks or sex is illegal discrimination in the EU and the US, and it's also shallow strategy. But the question underneath it is useful: *what about the customer-facing experience makes people want to come back and spend?* For martial arts schools, the answer is trust, warmth, and feeling safe, and your AI agent is often the school's first impression.

- **The parent of a bullied child** needs a calm, patient voice that says "you're in the right place" before any talk of pricing.
- **An adult woman asking about self-defense** may want to know about the class atmosphere or whether women train there. An agent that handles this sensitively wins that membership.
- **A 40-year-old former student** asking about getting back into training needs to hear it's not too late, and an agent with high-energy sales talk may lose him.
- **The practical application:** test voice choice, pace, and tone with real parents, and notice which calls convert. That's the legitimate version of the author's point.

## 7. "Your analysis will get better and it'll get more precise"

This part matters, and it only happens if you check your guesses against reality. I'd also reframe "never turn your brain off the money." Constant rumination tends to wear down judgment and relationships. What builds the skill is deliberate practice: regular, structured reps with a feedback loop.

- **Build a calibration loop with clients.** Before a discovery call, write down your estimate of the school's student count, monthly revenue, and missed calls per week. Then ask them. After 10 schools, your guesses will be sharp enough to impress owners in the first five minutes.
- **Do a daily 10-minute rep.** Pick one business you touched that day, whether a café, a gym, or an online order, and run a checklist below in your notes app.
- **Do a weekly deep teardown.** Fully mystery-shop one martial arts school: phone, website, social, and reviews. By the end of a year you'll have 50 teardowns, more field data than almost anyone selling to this industry.
- **Do a monthly competitor check.** Sign up for, or request a demo of, one competing AI receptionist or school software product, and note what they do better than you.

The core idea in that text is not actually "think about money all the time."

The useful business principle is:

> Train yourself to see every interaction as a business system with customers, economics, incentives, operations, marketing, and bottlenecks.

For your business (AI phone agents for martial arts schools), the highest-value version of this exercise is not analyzing coffee shops. It's analyzing every customer acquisition, sales, retention, and operational process that touches martial arts schools.
