## The part of that text that actually matters

The Telegram story isn't about Telegram. Strip it down and there are three mechanics:

1. **Ideas have no defense.** He saw a free group, he ran a free group. Nothing stopped him.
2. **He won on promotion volume, not originality.** Six posts a day with staff retweeting, versus the originator's one. Same idea, 5x the result.
3. **Blocking has value even when you lose.** The $50k GoFundMe is a win because it's $50k the competitor didn't get.

Everything below applies those three to AI phone agents for martial arts schools.

---

## 1. You can't steal what you can't see — build the surveillance layer first

He says "keep an eye on competitors." Most people mean that vaguely. Make it mechanical:

- **Meta Ad Library.** Free, public, shows every ad your competitors run right now. Search every AI-receptionist company and every martial arts marketing agency. You'll see their hooks, their offers, their creative, and which ads they've kept running for 90+ days (that's the one that works).
- **Alias inbox on every list.** Sign up for every competitor's newsletter, free trial, and lead magnet with a dedicated email. You get their nurture sequence, their launch timing, their discounts.
- **Book their demo.** Watch how they pitch, what objections they pre-handle, what they charge, what they claim about call handling.
- **Live in the same rooms your buyers do.** The martial arts school owner Facebook groups, BJJ gym owner groups, Reddit, the MAIA orbit. Don't post yet. Watch who posts what and what gets comments.
- **Website change alerts.** Visualping on their pricing page, homepage, and integrations page. A pricing change or a new integration logo is a roadmap leak.
- **Their job board.** Hiring bilingual voice engineers means Spanish-language agents are coming. Hiring SDRs means they're going outbound.
- **Sponsor lists and speaker lineups.** Martial Arts SuperShow, NAPMA events, the gym-owner podcasts. Who's paying to be in front of your buyer.

Cadence: 30 minutes, Monday morning, every week. Log it somewhere with four columns: what they did, why they did it, our version, ship date.

## 2. Steal the mechanism, not the artifact

This is where people get it wrong and create a legal problem instead of a business. The distinction is idea versus expression.

**Free to take:** offer structure, pricing model, guarantee, channel choice, content format, launch timing, onboarding flow, feature concepts, partnership types.

**Will cost you:** their ad copy word-for-word, their landing page code, their brand name or a confusable version of it, screenshots of their product, anything an ex-employee of theirs brings you under NDA.

Practical version: competitor runs "Never miss another call — 14-day free trial, we set it up for you." You don't copy that sentence. You copy the structure (risk reversal + done-for-you setup) and write it in your own voice, aimed harder: "We'll set it up on your school's line in 20 minutes. If it doesn't book a trial class in 14 days, don't pay."

## 3. The 48-hour rule, and the kit that makes it possible

His whole advantage was that he moved while the other guy slept. Speed isn't a personality trait, it's prepared infrastructure. Build a launch kit now so copying costs you hours, not weeks:

- A landing page template you can re-skin in an hour
- A checkout link and a webinar registration flow already wired
- An email sequence skeleton with the school-owner language already written
- A designer or VA on retainer who can turn work around same day
- A short-form video setup that doesn't require scheduling a shoot

Then apply it:

- **They launch a free Facebook group for school owners.** You launch yours inside 48 hours, seed it with five owner friends on day one, post three times a day, run a weekly live Q&A where you take real calls. Same idea, five times the activity, exactly like his Telegram group.
- **They post a "missed calls are costing you money" calculator.** You ship yours in a day, with better math, and it emails a personalized one-page PDF with their school's name on it.
- **They announce an integration with one school CRM.** You announce three, and you announce it in the groups where their customers hang out.
- **They put out a case study.** You put out five, and yours have the actual call audio.

## 4. Out-promote, don't just out-build

The Telegram group won because of six posts a day. Copying the asset is half the job; the other half is intensity.

One webinar should become: 15 short clips, 5 emails, a post in eight owner groups, 200 personalized DMs, a podcast pitch, and paid spend behind whichever clip performs. Competitors typically post once and move on.

Specific to phone agents: your unfair promotional asset is **audio**. Publish a live number people can call to talk to your agent right now. Post real recordings of the agent booking a trial class at 9pm on a Sunday. Screenshots don't compete with a school owner hearing their own objection handled.

And the highest-leverage version: get your customers posting it. A school owner saying "this booked four trials while I was teaching" in a group you're not even in beats anything you post yourself.

## 5. Counter-programming — deny them free rein

This is his GoFundMe logic, and it works here for a specific structural reason: your buyer pool is finite. There are maybe 30,000 US martial arts schools, and the active owners congregate in the same handful of groups, the same few events, the same podcasts. Attention is a fixed pool. Every slot you occupy is one they don't.

- **They run a webinar Tuesday 7pm ET.** You run a free "AI phone agent teardown" the same evening, or you post a comparison breakdown that morning so people arrive at their webinar already skeptical.
- **They exhibit at the SuperShow and you can't afford a booth.** Run a happy hour at the same hotel, sponsor the lanyards or the coffee, run geo-fenced ads inside the convention center, blitz the attendee list on DM.
- **They announce Black Friday pricing.** You announce yours a day earlier with a longer window.
- **They sponsor the big gym-owner podcast.** You sponsor three smaller ones the same month and get more total impressions cheaper.
- **They launch an affiliate program for martial arts consultants.** You launch one with a better split and sign the consultants first.

## 6. The upgrade the text doesn't give you: steal sideways

Direct competitors are the smallest pool of ideas available to you. The big steals come from verticals that are two years ahead of yours:

- **AI receptionists for dental and med spa.** They've already solved "no-show recovery," "insurance question deflection," and the two-ring answer guarantee. Rename it for trials and belt testing.
- **Auto dealership AI phone companies.** Their whole pitch is missed-call revenue math. Their ROI calculators and case study formats transfer directly.
- **Restaurant AI phone tools.** They pioneered the call-this-number-to-hear-it-live demo.
- **Home services AI SDR playbooks.** Speed-to-lead under 60 seconds as the headline metric.
- **Martial arts marketing agencies.** Steal their *offer* structures (paid trial funnels, six-week programs) and become the phone layer that makes their funnels work instead of competing with them.

Nobody in your niche will notice, and it's a deeper well.

## 7. Where I'd calibrate this for your specific market

A few places following the text literally would cost you:

**This niche is small and gossipy.** Martial arts school owners and the vendors who serve them all know each other. Copy the mechanism, change the skin. Visibly cloning a named person's post the day after they publish it gets screenshotted, and in a community this tight that reputation hit costs more than the tactic gains.

**Only block with moves you'd be happy to own anyway.** He can afford to spend purely to deny someone attention. You probably can't. Filter every blocking move: would I run this if the competitor didn't exist? If yes, run it on their launch day and get the block for free. If no, you're spending scarce cash on spite.

**"Steal everything" will shred your team.** Filter by whether it touches lead generation, conversion, or retention. If it doesn't, log it and move on. A competitor rebranding their logo is not a thing to copy.

**Don't counter-program people you need.** School management platforms, event organizers, associations, big consultants. Those are distribution partners disguised as adjacent competitors.

**Copying keeps you level, it doesn't build a moat.** This playbook is an offense system, not a defense one. What actually makes you hard to displace in vertical AI is switching cost: deep CRM integration, months of that school's call history training the agent, their scripts and pricing baked in, the front desk staff trained on it. Run the steal playbook to win deals, but spend a fixed share of your build time on the things a competitor can't copy in 48 hours.

## Your first 30 days

Week 1: build the monitoring stack (ad library, alias inbox, alerts, groups, demo bookings). Make the steal board.

Week 2: build the launch kit so your copy time drops to hours.

Week 3: pick the three highest-value things you found and ship your version of each, with 5x the promotion the originator gave it.

Week 4: map competitor launch dates, webinars, and event presence for the next quarter and put your counter-programming on the calendar now.

Then it runs on a loop: watch Monday, ship by Wednesday, out-promote through the weekend.

The core idea in that passage is **not actually "theft" in a legal sense**. It's:

> Watch what is already working in the market, copy the underlying strategy quickly, execute harder, distribute better, and learn faster than competitors.

For an AI phone-agent business serving martial arts schools, there is a lot you can take from this philosophy, and a few parts you should *not* take literally.
