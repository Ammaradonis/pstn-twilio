The core idea in that text is sound, and it fits a subscription business like yours even better than it fit T2. One part I'd leave behind, though: the invented McDonald's airtime story and the junk overnight spots. The 65% upsell rate didn't come from the lie. It came from people loving their advert. That's the part worth copying. Faking a reason is especially risky in your niche, because school owners talk at seminars, in association groups, and at tournaments, and on recurring billing one burned client costs you every future month plus their referrals. You have plenty of real reasons to sell, so everything below uses those.

## 1. Sell after "the advert moment," never before

**From the text:** "We never tried to sell it to them before they saw the advert because then it was much harder to sell." And the 65% only happened "IF people liked their advert."

For you, the advert is the moment a school owner *experiences* the agent working. Your job is to create that moment on purpose and put your offer right after it. Examples of your advert moments:

- The owner hears a call recording where the agent booked a trial at 9:40pm while he was teaching the adult class.
- A 30-day report shows how many calls were answered, how many came after hours, and how many trials were booked.
- A parent walks in for a trial and says "your receptionist on the phone was so helpful."
- The first Monday after a weekend when calls used to go to voicemail and now there are three trials on the calendar.

**Implementation:** Build a "results review" call around day 30. Show the numbers, play the two best recordings, let them react, and only *then* introduce the add-on. If they're not impressed yet, you don't pitch. You fix.

## 2. Keep the front-end offer lean

**From the text:** "If we tried to put that extra package in the front end and sell 24, 25 grand packages, we couldn't sell it."

Every feature you bundle into the first sale makes the first yes harder. Hold things back for later:

- Sell the core promise ("never miss a call, book more trials"), not reactivation campaigns, texting, and reviews all at once.
- With a multi-location owner, start with the flagship school, not all four.
- Don't push annual billing upfront. Offer annual prepay at month two or three, once they trust it.
- Save a second-language line or custom voice for after they've seen the basic agent work.

## 3. Design add-ons that have real margin and real value

**From the text:** "We'd sell it for 5 grand and it had 4 grand profit in it for us. That's how we were surviving."

T2 got margin by selling cheap airtime. You get margin because once software is built, delivering it again costs little. Add-ons that fit martial arts schools:

- **Former-student reactivation:** the agent calls people who quit in the last year or two with a comeback offer.
- **Trial no-show rebooking:** the agent follows up with everyone who booked and didn't show.
- **Trial reminders and confirmations** to cut no-shows before they happen.
- **Seasonal campaign lines** for back-to-school, New Year, summer camp registration, and birthday parties.
- **Review requests** after belt gradings, when families are proudest.
- **Additional locations** or a second-language line.

Price each one against the owner's own numbers. If one new student paying monthly tuition covers the add-on, that's an easy decision for them.

## 4. Only resell people you "relatively fulfilled"

**From the text:** "As long as you relatively fulfilled your deal," and "if you do a good job or a half good job."

Upselling an unhappy client speeds up churn. Sort your client list into three groups before you pitch anyone:

- **Green:** the agent books trials and the owner is happy. Pitch them now.
- **Yellow:** it works, but the owner hasn't looked at the results, or something's off. Maybe the agent quotes an outdated class schedule, or the owner doesn't realize the new trials came from the agent. Fix that and run the results review first.
- **Red:** complaints, low usage, or script problems. Fix these and don't pitch. Your goal with a red is to turn them yellow.

## 5. Your list is money sitting there, on two levels

**From the text:** "Bro, these customers are 200 grand... your money is sitting right there, go get it."

**Level one is your schools.** If you have 30 clients and 20 are green, and half of those take a reactivation add-on, that's 10 sales with zero cold calls and zero ad spend.

**Level two is your schools' own lists,** and this is where it gets powerful. Every school owner is the guy with 10,000 customers who thinks he needs a loan. They're sitting on:

- Hundreds of former students from the last five years.
- Trial bookings that never converted, including last year's back-to-school leads.
- Kids who finished the little-ones program and never moved up to juniors.
- Families who booked a birthday party but never enrolled.

So the pitch writes itself: "You don't need more ads. You've got hundreds of people who already trusted you once. Let the agent call them." You're applying the principle to your list by selling a tool that applies it to theirs.

## 6. Give existing customers a deal the public doesn't get

**From the text:** "Let the public pay full price. Only these people who bought from you before deserve the discount."

This is also your fastest cash lever. Examples:

- **Annual prepay for current clients only,** at a discount off monthly. This brings months of revenue in now, exactly what the text means by "pulling the ace from your sleeve."
- **Client-first pricing** on a new feature for 30 days before it's offered publicly.
- **A locked-in beta rate** for new capabilities as you build them.
- **Referral credit:** a free month for each school they bring in, so your happy clients sell for you.

Keep the deadlines and the price gap real, and honor them. That's what keeps the next offer credible.

## 7. Make it your first reflex, not a last resort

**From the text:** "Don't look at advertising more, don't look at getting new customers, don't look at a new product launch... is there anything I've already got that I can resell?"

Whenever you want more cash, ask three questions before anything else: What do my green clients not have yet? Who's on monthly billing that would prepay? What real seasonal moment is coming up for schools?

Here's how that reflex changes decisions:

- Instead of "run ads to find new schools," you send a client-only annual prepay offer.
- Instead of "build a new product," you package the reactivation calling the agent can already do.
- Instead of "borrow money or raise," you work the list first.

The calendar gives you honest urgency all year. It's mid-September now, so the obvious next hook is January, one of the biggest enrollment windows for schools. "If you want the agent calling your former students the first week of January, we need to set it up in November" is true, specific, and needs no made-up McDonald's deal.

## A 30-day starting plan

In week one, pull results for every client and tag them green, yellow, or red. In week two, pick one add-on (a New Year reactivation campaign is a strong first choice), set the price, and write your results-review script. In week three, book results reviews with every green and fix the yellows. In week four, send the client-only annual prepay offer with a real deadline.

The core lesson is **not** the deceptive parts ("make some shit up," fake scarcity, hiding low-quality inventory). Those tactics create short-term revenue and long-term trust damage.

The lesson worth internalizing is:

> **The cheapest customer acquisition cost is often zero.**
>
> Someone who has already bought from you, gotten value, and trusts you is dramatically easier to sell to than a stranger.

For your business—**AI phone agents for martial arts schools**—this principle can be extremely powerful because most schools have ongoing operational problems, not one-time problems.
