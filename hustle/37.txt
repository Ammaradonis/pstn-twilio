## 1. The reframe, translated into your business

"You're not making shit, you're taking shit" means something specific for you: every euro that hits your account came out of a martial arts school owner's account, and it was already spoken for. That money was going to be rent, mat insurance, a part-time front desk kid, a Facebook ads guy, a coaching program, or into the owner's own pocket to pay his mortgage.

So the real question is never "how do I make €5k/month?" It's "whose budget line am I taking €297/month out of, and what were they going to spend it on instead?"

Three concrete answers for a typical school:

- **The front desk wage line.** A school paying a part-time parent or a brown belt to sit at the desk 4–9pm is already spending real money to answer phones badly. You're taking part of that.
- **The ad spend line.** A school running €800/month in Meta ads where 40% of the calls go to voicemail during class time is burning money. You're taking your fee out of the waste, not out of new budget.
- **The consultant/coaching line.** Most school owners are already paying someone monthly for "more students." You're competing with that guy for the same drawer of cash.

If you can't name which drawer you're reaching into, your pricing and your pitch will both be vague.

## 2. "Money is like water" — map the flows in a dojo

The point of the water story is positioning, not philosophy. Stand where it's already moving. Money is already moving through a martial arts school in predictable channels:

- **Monthly memberships** flowing in from parents, mostly kids programs.
- **Ad budget** flowing out to Meta, Google, and whoever manages it.
- **Labor** flowing out to front desk staff and assistant instructors.
- **Software** flowing out to Zen Planner, Kicksite, Spark, Gymdesk, PushPress, whatever they run.
- **Coaching/franchise fees** flowing out to their mentorship program, association, or franchisor.

Now, where do you stand to get wet? A few positions, and they are not equally good:

- **Stand next to the ad spend.** Pitch yourself as the thing that stops leads leaking. You get paid out of money already committed, which is the easiest sale in the list.
- **Stand next to the front desk wage.** Pitch as a cheaper, never-sick, never-quits alternative for the phone specifically. Same logic: existing budget.
- **Stand upstream, next to the people who already have the schools.** This is the big one people miss. Instead of selling one school at a time, go to where hundreds of schools' money already pools: martial arts marketing agencies, school-management software companies, franchise groups and associations, and the big coaching programs. One white-label deal with an agency that manages 60 schools puts you in a much faster-moving river than 60 cold calls.
- **Stand next to the birthday parties and after-school programs.** Schools with after-school pickup programs have the highest-value inbound calls and the least phone coverage, because the owner is driving a van at 3pm.

Pick the position deliberately. Right now you're probably standing at the slowest part of the river, which is cold outreach to individual owners.

## 3. "Everyone's a middleman" — be honest about what you're in the middle of

You are a middleman twice over, and knowing both changes what you do.

**Middleman #1: between a parent's intent and the school's ability to capture it.** A mum decides at 9:40pm that her 7-year-old is getting bullied and needs confidence. She searches, finds three schools, calls one. Nobody answers. That intent evaporates. You stand between that moment and the school's calendar. That's your actual product: captured intent, converted into a booked trial.

**Middleman #2: between a telephony/voice stack and a school owner.** You buy minutes and model calls wholesale and sell them with a wrapper. Say it plainly to yourself, because it tells you exactly where your margin lives. It does not live in the tech, which anyone can buy. It lives in the wrapper:

- Scripts that know the difference between a Little Dragons enquiry and an adult Muay Thai enquiry.
- Objection handling for "how much is it?" that books the trial instead of quoting a price.
- Integration that actually writes the trial into Kicksite or Spark, not just an email summary.
- Knowing that the highest-value call of the week is a parent asking about the after-school program.

Two schools' worth of that knowledge is worth more than any model you plug in. That's the difference between a middleman with margin and a middleman getting squeezed.

## 4. Do the exercise properly

He's right that this is the whole thing. Here it is filled in for you, and then the part he skipped.

**I have this:** an AI phone agent that answers every call during class hours and after hours, and books trial classes directly into the school's system.

**I'm selling to this person:** an owner-operator of a single-location school doing maybe 80–150 students, who teaches on the mat from 4pm to 9pm and runs paid ads.

**They will give me their money because:**

- He is physically on the mat holding pads when the phone rings. He cannot answer it. This is not a preference problem, it's a physics problem.
- The parent calling him is calling two other schools in the same ten minutes. Whoever picks up gets the trial.
- Parents research after their kids are in bed. Calls come at 9pm and Sunday afternoon, when nobody is at the desk.
- His front desk staff turns over constantly and has no sales training, so even answered calls don't get booked.
- He's already paying for the leads. A missed call is money he's already spent, thrown away.
- One recovered enrollment covers your fee for months, because a kids membership is worth well over a thousand in lifetime value.
- He gets a text or dashboard showing exactly who called and what happened, which he currently has zero visibility into.
- It removes an argument with his wife, who currently answers the phone from home.

Now the part the text leaves out: **write down why they will NOT give you their money**, because that's what your marketing actually has to break.

- "I'm not letting a robot talk to parents about their kids." Trust is the entire product in martial arts. This is the number one objection and it's emotional, not logical.
- "My software already does missed-call text-back."
- "I got burned by a marketing guy last year."
- "I'm not techy, this sounds like a project."
- "€297 a month is two students, I'd rather keep it."

Every one of these needs a specific answer, and the first one needs a demo, not an argument. Which leads to the marketing change.

## 5. What actually changes in your marketing

The text promises this reframe changes how you sell. Here's what it should change concretely:

- **Stop selling "AI phone agent."** Nobody has a budget line called AI. Sell "booked trial classes from the calls you're missing." That's a line they already fund.
- **Lead with their own numbers, not yours.** Ask for their call log or Meta lead count and count the misses with them. You're not describing a problem, you're showing them money leaving the building. That's the "taking" made visible.
- **Demo before pitch.** Build the agent for their school, with their program names and schedule, and give them the number. Let them call it. The robot objection dies on contact with a good demo and survives any amount of copywriting.
- **Price against the drawer you're raiding.** If you're taking from the front desk wage, price under it and say so. If you're taking from wasted ad spend, price as a percentage of the waste.
- **Go where schools already pool.** One conversation with a martial arts marketing agency, a franchise group, or a software vendor is worth thirty cold DMs to individual owners.

## 6. One correction, because it will cost you money

The "money is only taken" framing is excellent marketing discipline and bad economics, and the difference matters for your specific business.

Trade isn't zero-sum. When the school pays you €297 and gets €2,000 in recovered enrollments, both of you are better off. That's why the deal renews. If you internalize "take" too literally, you'll optimize for extraction: hard closes, lock-in contracts, hiding churn. In an industry this small and this word-of-mouth driven, where owners all know each other from seminars and tournaments and association Facebook groups, that will kill you inside a year.

Use "take" for the diagnosis. Use "create" for the delivery. The sentence that holds both: *the money has to come out of someone's account, so I'd better be putting more back into it than I'm taking out.*

The most useful way to internalize this idea is to **translate it from its provocative wording into a business principle that is actually actionable**.

The literal claim "money can only be taken" is incomplete. In functioning markets, money is usually **exchanged**, not merely taken. However, the underlying lesson is powerful:

> Money flows from people and organizations that perceive enough value to voluntarily part with it.

For your business (AI phone agents for martial arts schools), this perspective can dramatically improve your positioning, sales process, and marketing.
