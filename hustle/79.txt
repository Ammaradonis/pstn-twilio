The core idea in that text is simple: **people don't buy solutions to problems they don't know they have.** The cam-site guy was "fine until you told him there's a problem." Most martial arts school owners are in exactly that state. They think their phone situation is fine because nobody complains about a call that never got answered. Your job is to make that invisible problem visible, then hand them the fix immediately.

Here's how each part of the text maps to your business.

---

## 1. "He was fine until YOU told him there's a problem"

Missed calls are invisible. The owner is on the mat teaching from roughly 4–8pm, which is exactly when parents call after work and school. Those parents don't complain. They just call the next school. So the owner genuinely believes nothing is wrong.

Ways to make it visible:

- **Mystery call:** Call their school at 5:45pm on a weekday. If it goes to voicemail, open with: *"I called your school Tuesday at 5:45 as a parent. Nobody picked up. How many other parents do you think did the same thing that week?"*
- **Call log check:** *"Can you pull up your phone log for last week and count the missed calls between 4 and 8pm?"* Most owners have never looked.
- **Voicemail ratio:** *"Of those missed calls, how many left a voicemail?"* Let them discover that most didn't.

## 2. The pen and the bouncy ball: every feature needs a problem attached

"If you drop your ball, it breaks, but my ball bounces." A feature means nothing until it's tied to something going wrong. Adapt this to whatever your agent actually does:

| Feature | The problem it kills |
|---|---|
| Answers 24/7 | A parent researching classes at 10pm after their kids are asleep hits voicemail and books with the school that answered. |
| Answers during class | You either stop teaching to grab the phone (in front of students and watching parents) or you miss the call. |
| Books trials directly | "We'll call you back" means the lead goes cold. By the time you call after class, they've moved on. |
| Same script every time | Your teenage front desk helper says "uh, I think it's like $150?" and the parent loses confidence. |

**Drill:** Write down every feature you have, and next to each, write the pain it prevents in the owner's own language.

## 3. The valve: "You're wasting money"

The oil guy didn't argue about valves. He made it about money leaking out. Your version is lifetime student value. Use **their** numbers, not yours:

> Missed calls per month × % who'd have enrolled × monthly tuition × months a student stays

Example: 20 missed calls a month, 1 in 10 would have enrolled, $150/month, students stay 18 months. That's 2 students × $150 × 18 = **$5,400 in lost revenue every month**. Suddenly your monthly fee looks tiny.

Other "wasting money" angles:

- **Ad spend:** *"You spent $1,200 on Facebook ads last month. How many of those leads called and got voicemail? You paid for them and then let them ring out."*
- **Staff cost:** *"You're paying someone part-time to sit at the desk, and they still can't cover evenings and weekends."*
- **Owner time:** *"How many hours a week do you spend playing phone tag? What's your hourly rate as the head instructor?"*

## 4. The TV ad: "How can you be the premier brand if…"

This is a different kind of problem. It's about identity and status, not money. Some owners respond more to this than to math.

- *"Parents judge your school before they ever see your mat. The first impression is the phone."*
- *"The franchise down the road has someone answering every call. A mom calls both of you. Who wins?"*
- *"When you answer mid-class with kids yelling in the background, what does that tell a parent about how organized your program is?"*
- *"You can't grow to 300 students if you're personally answering every call."*

## 5. The cam girl move: ask, don't tell

This is the most useful part of the text. She didn't announce a problem. She asked a question ("When was the last time…?") and whatever he answered confirmed it. People believe what they say out loud far more than what you tell them.

Diagnostic questions for school owners:

- *"Who answers the phone at 6pm on a Tuesday while you're teaching the kids' class?"*
- *"When a lead comes in from your website at 9pm, how fast does someone call them back?"*
- *"What happens to calls on Sundays?"*
- *"If I called your school right now pretending to be a parent, what would happen?"*

And as the text says, it doesn't matter what they answer:

- "My wife answers." → *"What happens when she's busy with dinner or the kids?"*
- "We call back the next day." → *"How many of those parents have already booked a trial somewhere else by then?"*
- "We don't miss calls." → *"Great. Want to test it? I'll call Thursday at 6:15 and we'll see."*

## 6. "Couple that with the WHY"

Once the problem lands, tie it to why they opened the school in the first place:

- **Purpose:** *"You didn't open a dojo to be a receptionist. Every hour on the phone is an hour you're not teaching."*
- **Mission:** *"Every parent who can't get through is a kid who never gets the confidence your program gives."*
- **Family:** *"You're answering inquiry calls at your own dinner table."*
- **Financial:** *"This is the difference between the school surviving and you finally paying yourself properly."*

## 7. "Instantly solve": make the solution immediate

Don't let the problem sit. The moment it lands, hand them the fix so they can feel it right away:

- *"Grab your phone. Call this number and pretend you're a mom asking about classes for your 7-year-old."* A live demo is your strongest closer, because it solves the exact problem they just admitted to.
- *"We can have this answering your phones by next week, so this Tuesday's 6pm calls get picked up."*
- *"Let's run it for 30 days and compare your booked trials to last month."* (Only if you can actually offer that.)

## 8. "Tell the mass market they have a big issue"

Use the same problem-first framing in your outreach:

- **Cold email subject:** "I called your school at 6:15 last night"
- **Facebook ad hook:** "Your phone rings during kids' class. Who answers it?"
- **Short video:** Call a few schools at 6pm (keep them anonymous) and count how many go to voicemail.
- **At tournaments or seminars:** *"Quick question: who's answering your school's phone right now?"* They're at the tournament, so the answer is nobody.

## The part I'd leave out

Two lines in that text will hurt you: "Even if the private is the same, who cares?" and the valve guy insisting "yes you do" without proof. Inventing problems or overpromising might close one deal, but you sell a monthly subscription. If an owner signs because you claimed he's losing 40 calls a week and he isn't, he cancels in month two. Martial arts owners are also a tight community. They talk at seminars and in owner groups, so one burned client travels.

The good news is that you rarely need to invent anything. Missed calls during class are close to universal for small schools, because the owner teaches during peak calling hours. So the rule is: **reveal the problem with their own data, don't fabricate it.** A mystery call and their own call log are more convincing than any line you could make up.

## How to internalize it this week

1. **Build a problem bank.** Write 15 problems a school owner has, phrased the way they'd say them.
2. **Do 10 mystery calls** to schools in the 5–7pm window and note what happens. That gives you real stories to open with.
3. **Memorize four diagnostic questions** from section 5 until you can ask them naturally.
4. **Practice the demo handoff** so you can go from "problem admitted" to "call this number" in under 10 seconds.
5. **Record your sales calls** and check whether you led with the problem or with your features. If you started with features, redo the pitch.

The core idea in that transcript is **problem → consequence → solution**.

What makes it powerful is not the manipulation aspect. It's that people usually buy when they clearly understand:

1. What problem exists.
2. Why that problem matters.
3. What it is costing them.
4. Why your solution is the fastest or best fix.

Instead of telling them they have a problem, lead them to discover it.

"Every inquiry gets answered immediately, booked, and followed up automatically."
