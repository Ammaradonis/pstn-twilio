## First, the quote he half-remembered

It was Patton. The actual line is "A good plan violently executed now is better than a perfect plan executed next week." The word he dropped is **violently**. That word is doing most of the work. Patton isn't saying mediocre plans are fine. He's saying the energy you'd spend perfecting the plan should be redirected into executing the decent one harder. Keep that version, because it protects you from the lazy reading of this lesson ("anything counts").

---

## Why it actually works (this is the part to internalize)

Don't internalize it as motivation. Internalize it as three mechanics.

**1. Every action returns information. Inaction returns nothing.**

You send a cold email to a BJJ gym owner and get no reply. You now know: that subject line, that gym type, that send time, dead. That's data you didn't have at 9am. Run 40 of those and you have a map. Sit and think about the perfect email for three days and you have an opinion.

Applied to you:
- Call 10 martial arts schools at 7:15pm as a fake parent. Six won't pick up. Now you don't *believe* schools miss calls, you *know*, with numbers, and you have a sales asset.
- Change your pricing page from $297 to "starts at $197" for a week. Either demos go up or they don't. Two weeks of debating it produces zero bits of information.
- Let your agent handle 50 live calls with an imperfect script. You'll discover the top 5 questions parents actually ask, which are never the 5 you guessed. (It's going to be: "how old does my kid have to be," "what's the price," "do I need a uniform," "can we try a free class," "what time is the kids class.")

**2. Motion is the only thing that breaks the paralysis state.**

Deliberation feels like progress and produces none. The uncle's insight wasn't about clean walls. It was that a 15-year-old standing still develops the *identity* of someone who stands still. You don't think your way into moving. You move and your head follows.

**3. Compounding.**

One Instagram DM to a dojo owner is worthless. 50 a day for 60 days is 3,000 conversations, 300 replies, 30 demos, 6 clients, each worth $200-400/month recurring. The math only ever runs on actions taken.

## The one rule that lets you run at full speed safely

He says "it's not very often someone's gonna do something and catastrophically destroy your business." Mostly true, and here's the sharpened version that makes it *more* usable:

**Sort every action by blast radius and reversibility.**

**Two-way doors** (reversible, cheap): move instantly, never ask, never think. Emails, DMs, tweets, copy tweaks, a test price, a new script variant, a Loom, a cold call, a landing page, an outreach angle. These are the "wipe the wall" category. Go at 100% velocity with 60% quality.

**One-way doors** (irreversible, or burns a scarce resource): these get 20 minutes of thought, not three days.

For your specific business, the one-way doors are narrow but real:
- Blasting all 400 martial arts schools in your metro with a bad pitch in one day. That's your entire addressable market in one region, burned. Send 20, see what lands, then send 380.
- Getting banned from the 4 or 5 Facebook groups where martial arts school owners actually congregate (Martial Arts Business Owners, gym-management groups). Same for r/bjj and r/martialarts. His "post your link on Reddit 10,000 times" advice would get you permabanned in about 40 minutes, and there is no second Reddit. Post value 10 times, link once.
- Letting a broken agent answer live calls for a paying gym without telling them. One misquoted price to a parent and you lose the client and the referral.
- Signing an annual tool contract before you have 10 customers.

Everything else in your business is a two-way door. Which means roughly 95% of your day should be pure unthinking velocity, and 5% should be careful. Most people invert this and apply one-way-door caution to sending an email.

## The implementation: answer "like what?" once, in writing, forever

This is the actual system. The failure in the transcript is the employee saying "like what?" You answer that question one time, on a calm Sunday, in a document, and then you never ask it again. When you don't know what to do, you don't think. You open the list and execute the top item.

Build it in four tiers by the time you have available.

**Tier 1 — you have 5 minutes**
- Call 3 martial arts schools in a new area code, let it ring out, log the result in a spreadsheet
- Send one follow-up to a lead who ghosted 30+ days ago ("Still missing calls during evening classes?")
- Text an existing client: "What's one thing the agent got wrong this week?"
- Record a school's voicemail greeting into your swipe file of bad ones
- Reply to one comment in a martial arts owners group with something actually useful and no pitch
- Add one question to your agent's FAQ handling

**Tier 2 — you have 30 minutes**
- Record a 60-second Loom: you calling a specific gym's real number, it ringing out to voicemail, then the same call handled by your agent. Send it to that owner by name. This is the single highest-converting asset available to you and it takes 8 minutes to make. You can make 4 of them in this slot.
- 25 Instagram DMs to school owners
- Rewrite one objection handler ("we already have a front desk person")
- Post one data point publicly: "Called 20 karate schools at 6pm. 13 went to voicemail."
- Call 10 schools live and try to get past the front desk

**Tier 3 — you have 2 hours**
- Build the missed-call revenue calculator: average student LTV (a kid who stays 2 years at $150/mo is $3,600) × missed calls per month × close rate. Gym owners think in students, not in software costs. Make the page.
- Run the "We called 100 martial arts schools" study and publish it. It's a lead magnet, a PR hook, and a cold email opener at the same time.
- Record a full demo call and rebuild the intake flow around what you hear
- Write 3 case studies from existing clients with actual numbers

**Tier 4 — you're fried, it's 11pm, you can't do real work**
- Read 20 call transcripts from yesterday
- Clean the CRM
- Build tomorrow's call list of 50 schools with names and numbers pulled
- Tag which of your leads are BJJ vs kids-focused TKD vs MMA (they buy differently)

Print it. Tape it to the wall. The uncle's answer was "wipe the wall, sweep the floor" because he'd already decided in advance what the fallback was. Your business needs the same pre-decided fallback so the answer arrives in zero seconds. That's the tie back to lesson one: **deliberation is latency**. You don't get speed by thinking faster, you get it by having already decided.

## "There's always work to do" mapped to your company

The uncle's real claim is that no business ever reaches a finished state. Here's where yours is permanently unfinished, so you always know which drawer to open:

- **Product** — the agent's script is never done. Every call it fumbles is a script edit. Never zero of these.
- **Distribution** — you can always contact one more school. There are roughly 30,000+ martial arts schools in the US alone. You will not run out of people to call in your lifetime.
- **Proof** — you can always collect one more testimonial, screenshot, or recorded before/after.
- **Retention** — you can always call a current client. The one you haven't called in 3 weeks is the one about to churn.
- **Ops** — onboarding can always take one fewer step. Right now it probably takes 90 minutes. Get it to 20.

If you ever think "I've done my work today," you're in one of five drawers and forgot the other four.

## When it's quiet

That's the actual content of the dancing story, stripped of the delivery: **quiet is a signal to change the motion, not to stop it.** No inbound, no replies, no demos booked. The instinct is to wait. Waiting has never once fixed a quiet pipeline.

Quiet inbound means go outbound. Quiet outbound means change the channel. Quiet in every channel means change the offer.

- No email replies today? Switch to phone. Call the schools directly and pitch the owner.
- Phone not working? Go in person. There are martial arts schools within 20 minutes of you. Walk in at 4pm before kids class, ask for the owner, show them their own missed call.
- Nobody buying at $297/mo? Offer a free 14-day pilot where you eat the cost. You'll learn more in 14 days of a live deployment than 3 months of pitching.
- Cold outreach dead entirely? Go where they already gather: martial arts business podcasts, Kovar/Ultimate Martial Arts Business-type seminars, gym management software communities.

## Training your staff on it

Three rules, stated explicitly, in writing:

1. **Never bring me "what should I do." Bring me "I did X, was that right?"** The first sentence is banned. If they're stuck, they go to the Default Action List.
2. **60% is the pass mark on two-way doors.** Tell them directly: "I would rather you send a mediocre email than ask me about a good one." Then when they send a mediocre one, praise the sending before you correct the email. If you correct first, they'll stop sending.
3. **Hand them the one-way door list.** Three or four items, max. "Don't email more than 30 new schools without showing me. Don't change a paying client's live agent without telling them. Don't post a promo link in a community you haven't given value to first." Everything not on that list is pre-approved forever.

The reason this works is that it removes you as the bottleneck. A team that waits for you moves at your speed. A team with pre-authorization moves at their own.

## Scoreboard

Count actions, not outcomes, for the first 90 days. Outcomes lag and will demoralize you. Actions are fully in your control.

Daily minimums, written down, checked off:
- 50 outbound touches (calls, emails, DMs, walk-ins)
- 1 asset created (Loom, post, case study, page)
- 1 product improvement (script edit, flow fix)
- 1 current-client touch

Hit those four every weekday and the revenue question answers itself in about 8 weeks.

## The one failure mode to watch

Volume pointed at the wrong thing is a treadmill that feels like a sprint. Tweaking your website copy for the eleventh time is action, it's just action aimed at nothing, and it's seductive precisely because it's easy and visible.

The fix isn't to slow down. It's to ask one question before each block of work: **what's actually stopping this business right now?** If the answer is "nobody knows I exist," then copy tweaks are wall-wiping and calls are real work. If the answer is "people book demos and don't close," then more calls are wall-wiping and fixing your demo is real work. If it's "clients churn at month 3," then all outbound is wall-wiping.

Same velocity. Pointed at the constraint. That's the version of this lesson that builds a company instead of just keeping you busy.

The core idea in that lesson is not “be busy for the sake of being busy.”

The real lesson is:

> When you're uncertain, default to productive action instead of waiting for perfect clarity.

For an AI phone agent business serving martial arts schools, this principle is especially powerful because most growth comes from volume of experiments, conversations, and iterations—not from sitting and thinking.
